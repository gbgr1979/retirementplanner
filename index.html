    How this model works & important limitations

    All amounts are illustrative. Growth is smooth, not a market simulation. Lower/higher scenarios are sensitivity checks, not confidence ranges. Tax estimates hold the 2026/27 rules constant into the future; your actual tax and future legislation can differ.

    The recurring gross-withdrawal target uses a simple disclosed pot order, not an optimised or guaranteed sustainable strategy. No benefit entitlement, investment recommendation, lifetime allowance protection, inheritance-tax planning, salary sacrifice, tapered annual allowance or carry-forward calculation is provided. Pension and savings tax limitations appear with the results.

    Your saved JSON file is unencrypted and may contain sensitive financial information. Keep it somewhere safe. No accounts, cookies, tracking, browser autosave or financial-data upload are used.

    About this scenario

    These are estimates to compare, not a guaranteed safe income. Withdrawals are before tax; the amount available to spend may be lower.

    Your money basis

    How withdrawals work

    The target starts at your retirement month, not your partner’s. In total-income mode, gross State Pension and other pension income reduce the monthly amount needed from pots, never below zero. In pots-only mode, pension income is additional and does not reduce the chosen withdrawals. We divide the annual target by twelve and use accessible cash savings, then cash ISAs, investment ISAs and DC pensions for the remaining pot requirement. Within each type, your pots come before your partner’s, in their listed order. This is not a tax-optimised strategy.

    Successful scheduled withdrawals count towards that month’s gross target; larger scheduled amounts still happen in full. Transfers do not count. State/defined-benefit pension income is separate. Spending is compared with cash received, not deducted from pots a second time. Surplus withdrawn cash is not carried forward or reinvested.

    Amounts removed from pension pots may be taxed, so spendable cash can be lower. The chart money-display toggle does not change this target’s basis. Returns and tax estimates are assumptions, not guarantees.

    State Pension and future tax

    Enter each person’s own State Pension forecast and check their State Pension age. New guided plans include an editable full-rate illustration, not confirmed entitlement. The suggested claim month uses an approximate birth date and the current timetable, not a universal claim age. Check both against your circumstances. Forecasts are entered in projection-start pounds; the chosen increase runs from the plan start, not just the first payment. Weekly × 52 ÷ 12 is an average month, not an exact payment schedule.

    State Pension is taxable income of the person receiving it, although it is normally paid gross. The illustration freezes 2026/27 income-tax rules. Later changes, including the Pension Tax Allowance announced for April 2027, are not implemented. After-tax figures are incomplete estimates, not a guaranteed spendable income. Read the official tax-change explainer.

    Changing your retirement year

    The buttons keep the selected month and change one year at a time. Your partner’s retirement date, pension-income dates and scheduled events stay fixed.

    Dates set by guided setup move with retirement. After loading a saved plan, contribution and spending dates stay fixed: adjust them separately in the editor. Dates you explicitly change to match retirement then move with it.

    Your two pot values

    The end age is a planning assumption, not a prediction of death. These totals include accessible and locked pots for everyone included in the plan. They are not an after-tax inheritance estimate; remaining pensions may be taxed when withdrawn.

    Reading the result

    A withdrawal shortfall means accessible pots cannot meet that month’s target. Locked pension funds may still exist. Funding through the projection end does not guarantee that the income is sustainable. Save a plan file to keep your changes; this tab does not autosave.
